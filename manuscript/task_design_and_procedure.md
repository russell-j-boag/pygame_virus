# Virus discrimination task design and procedure

The primary between-participant manipulation was unaided classification reliability, with stimulus difficulty calibrated to target 65% or 90% accuracy. Both groups completed three consecutive 400-trial aided phases with advice reliabilities of 95%, 70%, and 95%. During the degraded phase, aid reliability therefore exceeded the lower group's target accuracy but fell below the higher group's target. Two 200-trial manual segments preceded and followed the aided block, respectively.

Group and D/J response-key mapping were counterbalanced in a repeating four-participant cycle determined by participant number. Block and phase order were fixed.

Participants classified samples as V-BLACK or V-WHITE according to which colour predominated among 3,600 moving dots within a circular region on a grey background. Colour proportions remained constant within trials; samples closer to equal proportions were harder. Black-majority and white-majority samples were selected with equal probability.

After instructions, participants completed 300 unaided calibration trials. An adaptive staircase increased difficulty after correct responses and decreased it after errors or missed deadlines. The mean and standard deviation of the departure from equal colour proportions over the final 150 trials defined the stimulus distribution for all subsequent blocks. Distribution parameters remained fixed, while individual stimuli varied. The session comprised 1,900 trials.

During aided trials, BLACK or WHITE advice appeared above the sample at stimulus onset. Advice correctness was sampled independently on each trial according to the current phase's reliability. Manual experimental trials displayed ##### instead. Participants were told that reliability could decline and recover and that correct classification remained their responsibility. Exact reliabilities and transition points were undisclosed; transitions occurred without announcements or questionnaires.

Each trial began with a 750-ms fixation cross, followed by the sample until a D/J response or the 5 s deadline. A countdown timer displayed the remaining time. Instructions emphasised speed and accuracy and described a performance-dependent bonus of up to $25. CORRECT, INCORRECT, or TOO SLOW feedback followed each trial. Participants pressed a key to continue, permitting breaks between trials. Choice, accuracy, and response time were recorded.

Participants rated their own accuracy after calibration and each manual segment using 0–100% sliders. After the complete 1,200-trial aided block, they rated aid accuracy on the same scale and completed six trust items rated from 1 (*strongly disagree*) to 5 (*strongly agree*). These ratings concerned the aided block as a whole. The final performance score summarised accuracy across the 1,600 experimental trials.
