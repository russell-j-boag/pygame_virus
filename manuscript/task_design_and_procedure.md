# Virus discrimination task design and procedure

The experiment crossed time pressure (high or low) with advice availability (manual or aided) within participants. High pressure imposed a 1 s response deadline and low pressure a 3 s deadline. Each of the four conditions comprised 400 trials. The assignment of aid reliability to pressure condition varied between participants: one group received advice with a 95% probability of being correct under high pressure and 65% under low pressure; the other group received the reverse assignment. Each participant therefore encountered both reliability levels, each at a different deadline.

The planned allocation for 96 participants crossed all 24 orders of the four experimental blocks with the two reliability assignments and two response-key mappings. This provided four participants per block order, 48 per reliability assignment, and one per complete allocation cell. Allocation followed a fixed sequence determined by participant number.

Participants classified simulated biological samples as V-BLACK or V-WHITE according to which colour predominated among 3,600 moving dots within a circular region on a grey background. Dot positions changed continuously, but colour proportions remained constant within each trial. Samples closer to equal proportions were more difficult. Black-majority and white-majority samples were selected with equal probability.

After receiving task instructions, participants completed one block of 300 unaided calibration trials under the 3 s deadline. An adaptive staircase targeted 77% accuracy, increasing difficulty after correct responses and decreasing it after errors or missed deadlines. The mean and standard deviation of the departure from equal colour proportions over the final 150 calibration trials defined the stimulus-difficulty distribution used in all four experimental blocks. These parameters remained fixed across both pressure conditions, while individual stimuli varied across trials. The session comprised 1,900 trials, including calibration.

In aided blocks, a textual recommendation, BLACK or WHITE, appeared at the top of the display simultaneously with the sample. Advice correctness was sampled independently on each trial according to the block's reliability. In manual experimental blocks, a string of hash symbols (#####) occupied the recommendation location.

Before the high-reliability block, participants were instructed: “In this block, although the automation is highly reliable, it is not perfect, and automation advice errors are unlikely but still possible.” Before the low-reliability block, they were instructed: “In this block, although the automation is reasonably reliable, it is not perfect, and automation advice errors may be relatively common.” Exact reliability percentages were not disclosed. Before both aided blocks, participants were reminded that correct classification remained their responsibility, including when the aid was incorrect.

Each trial began with a 750-ms fixation cross, followed by the sample until a response or the deadline. Participants responded using D and J; their assignments to V-BLACK and V-WHITE were counterbalanced. Before each block, participants were informed of the response deadline (1 or 3 s) and instructed to respond as accurately as possible within it. They were told that missed deadlines would count as incorrect responses. A countdown timer showed the remaining time within the trial. Instructions also described a performance-dependent bonus of up to $25. Responses were followed by CORRECT or INCORRECT feedback; missed deadlines produced TOO SLOW feedback. Participants pressed a key to continue, permitting breaks between trials. Response choice, accuracy, and response time were recorded.

After calibration and each manual block, participants estimated their own accuracy on a 0–100% slider. After each aided block, they estimated aid accuracy on the same scale and completed six trust items rated from 1 (*strongly disagree*) to 5 (*strongly agree*). At completion, participants received their percentage of correct responses across the four experimental blocks.
